Lee Places – Understanding Urban Atmosphere

Exploring public spaces in Amsterdam & Berlin

Quick info

  • Context: Urban Interaction Design Minor
  • Focus: Public spaces, human behavior, digital media

Project Context

During the Urban Interaction Design minor, I worked on the Lee Places project, where we researched locations in Amsterdam and Berlin that naturally invite people to stay, interact, or relax. The goal was to understand what makes a place socially comfortable, and how urban design influences human behavior.

Research Question

What makes a public space feel like a place where people want to stay rather than just pass through?

Research Methods

To understand these spaces, we combined different research approaches:

  • Field Observation: Observing how people moved through the space, where they stopped, and how they interacted with their surroundings.
  • Sensory Interviews: Asking questions focused on human senses, such as:
    • What do you see in this space?
    • What do you hear around you?
    • What do you feel when you stay here?
    • What do you smell or notice about the atmosphere?
  • Documentation: Recording videos, voice memos, photos (day and evening), and maps of the locations to capture how the atmosphere changes throughout the day.

Locations Studied

We explored several public spaces in Amsterdam and Berlin:

  • Amsterdam: Backside of Amsterdam Central Station, Entrepotdok & Artis Flamingos, Café Het Kleine Lokaal
  • Berlin: Berlin Hauptbahnhof, East Side Gallery, Treptower Park, Tempelhofer Feld

Key Observations

Berlin Hauptbahnhof: Although the station was busy, the atmosphere felt surprisingly open and social. We observed spontaneous interactions, street performances, and conversations between strangers. However, people working nearby preferred quieter locations with more greenery.

East Side Gallery: This space had a dual character. During the day, it was crowded and fast-paced, mostly filled with tourists. In the evening, it transformed into a calmer space where people lingered by the river, enjoying social interactions and relaxation.

Role of Digital Media

We also analyzed how digital technology interacts with urban space. Examples included:

  • QR codes explaining the murals at the East Side Gallery
  • Audio guides and digital storytelling
  • VR experiences recreating the Berlin Wall

This showed how digital layers can enhance physical spaces, making history and culture more accessible.

Key Insight

Across both cities, we discovered that Lee Places share common characteristics:

  • ✔ Presence of greenery or water
  • ✔ Opportunities for social interaction
  • ✔ Comfortable places to sit and stay
  • ✔ A sense of safety and openness
  • ✔ A balance between activity and calmness

Spaces lacking these elements often become pass-through spaces instead of gathering places.

Outcome

Our team translated our research into:

  • Urban analysis boards
  • Comparative maps
  • Videos documenting sensory experiences
  • Design insights for improving public spaces

Reflection

This project helped me understand how urban design directly influences human behavior. I learned that designing public space is not only about architecture or aesthetics — it’s about creating environments where people feel comfortable, connected, and engaged. By combining behavioral psychology, urban observation, and digital tools, I developed a stronger approach to human-centered urban design.
