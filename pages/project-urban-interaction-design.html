About the Minor

During the Urban Interaction Design minor, I explored how design can influence the way people experience and interact with cities. The program combined product design, digital technology, behavioral psychology, and urban design, allowing me to approach public spaces from multiple perspectives.

What I Explored

Throughout the minor, I worked on projects that examined how urban design decisions affect behavior and social interaction. Some key experiences included:

  • Designing interactive urban concepts using tools like the Micro:bit.
  • Applying behavioral psychology models such as COM-B and Kahneman’s System 1 & 2 to influence human behavior.
  • Observing public space through sensory research methods, interviews, and fieldwork.
  • Using iMotions eye-tracking technology to analyze how people visually experience urban environments.
  • Conducting research in Amsterdam and Berlin to compare how different cities shape social interaction.

Example Project: Lee Places (Amsterdam & Berlin)

One of the main projects of the minor was researching “Lee Places” — locations where people naturally feel comfortable staying, interacting, or relaxing. Together with my team, I studied public spaces in Amsterdam and Berlin through:

  • Interviews with local residents, workers, and visitors
  • Sensory-based observation (what people see, hear, feel, and smell)
  • Video documentation and voice memos
  • Mapping and spatial analysis
  • Comparing how places change throughout the day

This research revealed how atmosphere, greenery, social activity, and accessibility strongly influence whether people choose to stay in a place or just pass through it. Our findings were translated into visual boards, maps, and design proposals that illustrated how urban spaces can become more welcoming and socially engaging.

Skills I Developed

During the minor, I strengthened skills across multiple disciplines:

  • Research & Observation: Field research, sensory interviews, stakeholder analysis, urban space observation
  • Design & Prototyping: Concept development, urban sketching, spatial thinking, experience design
  • Technology & Data: Micro:bit interaction design, eye-tracking analysis with iMotions, understanding urban sensors
  • Collaboration & Communication: Team-based design processes, visual storytelling, presenting research insights

Reflection

This minor significantly changed how I see design. Before, I mainly approached projects from a problem-solution perspective. During the program, I learned to think more deeply about human behavior, emotions, and context. I now understand that designing cities is not only about buildings and infrastructure — it’s about shaping how people move, connect, and experience space. By combining psychology, design, and technology, I’m developing a design approach that focuses on creating meaningful and human-centered urban experiences.
